# Speedboat perf, 2026-09-16 — measure, then optimise

Follows `docs/speedboat/perf-baseline.md` (T205, 2026-09-14). Since that baseline
landed: T209 night LEDs + light pools + cockpit lights, the hull volume mask, W9
at-speed spray/hull-spray pools, B124 AO receiver MRT, B125 local-mirror
reflection + hull wake-elevation sampler, T208 dash, T212 audio.

**Headline: the race does not hold 30 fps, and the reason has MOVED since T205.**
The main 1080p scene pass is still 75–83% of GPU busy, but inside it the two
costs that matter are now the **ocean surface (≈9.7 ms)** and the **at-speed
spray pools (≈8.1 ms)** — W9 spray was near-free at T205 (0.13 ms) and is now
the joint-largest item in the frame. The planar reflection is the mirror image
of that: **0.3 ms of GPU for 219 draw calls and 3.5 ms of CPU.**

## 0. Machine and method

- Apple M3 Max (30-core GPU), macOS 26.3, Chrome 152, WebGPU on Metal.
- **Headless Chrome** (`--headless=new`, `--mute-audio`, DPR 1), page served from
  an rsync snapshot of the working tree on port 5199 so another agent's live
  edits could not move the numbers mid-run. GPU lock held for every window.
- **Machine was NOT idle.** `tests/raceAiSweep.ts` (the race-AI agent, CPU-only)
  held one core at 100% throughout, and the user's own Chrome was resident. The
  CPU columns are therefore an upper bound; the GPU columns are not affected by
  a CPU-only neighbour. Absolute fps is pessimistic, deltas are sound.
- Page: `speedboat.html?autopilot`, Tower Reach, 7 NPCs, quality `medium`
  (the shipped default), sea Calm, time 15:00 unless the row says Night.
- Instrumentation lives in scratch only, nothing in `src/`.

### Why the GPU numbers are a UNION, not a sum

Chrome/Metal returns pass intervals that **overlap heavily** — measured here, the
half-res reflection pass reports 83 ms of raw interval per frame inside a 35 ms
frame, and the bloom mips each report ~20 ms while nested inside the scene pass.
Summing per-pass durations (what `renderer.info.render.timestamp` and
`src/debug/gpuTimer.ts` both do) overstates the frame by 3–10×; that is the same
trap `docs/agent-browser-harness.md` §4 and `perf-baseline.md` §6 record.

This pass keeps the **raw begin/end** of every pass and reports:

- **GPU busy** = length of the UNION of all pass intervals in the frame;
- **exclusive charge** per pass = `end − max(begin, previous end)` in begin
  order, which partitions the union exactly (T205's attribution, so the columns
  below are comparable with the baseline);
- raw duration alongside, kept but never summed.

Cross-check: GPU busy tracks frame time closely in every window below, and the
`ovf` (pool overflow) count was 0 in every window — no pass went untimed.

Notes.

- **Not vsync-quantised.** Headless presents offscreen without a 60 Hz lock, so
  frame time reads as real work (T205's 33/50 ms alternation is absent). Frame
  p50 tracks `max(GPU busy, CPU)` plus a few ms of submit overhead.
- **GPU is the wall in the race**: busy 27–42 ms against CPU 22–28 ms in every
  race window. In free roam the two converge (22.8 vs 14.6) because the seven
  rivals and their spray are gone.
- **Far chase is the worst case, not the pack start** — 41.5 ms busy. A far
  camera puts more ocean and more island in frame; the pack itself is cheaper
  than the water around it.
- **Night is the CHEAPEST race window** (27.3 ms) despite the most draws (602).
  T209's LEDs and light pools cost draw calls and CPU, not fill: the sun is
  down, so the ocean's sun-glint, sparkle and god-ray work collapses.
- 4K-ish costs +39% GPU over 1080p for +145% pixels — the frame is only
  partly fill-bound, which matches T205's 720p finding.

## 2. GPU ranking

### 2a. By pass (exclusive charge, share of GPU busy)

Consistent across every window:

| pass | pack chase | pack far | night | share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **main HDR scene pass 1920×1080 MSAA 4×** (`output`) | 28.8 | 34.6 | 20.5 | **75–83%** |
| compute groups (FFT, foam, flowfoam, wake, spray emit) | 3.8 | 4.1 | 4.2 | 10–15% |
| planar reflection 960×540 + its mip chain (14 passes) | 1.4 | 1.5 | 1.5 | 4–6% |
| `ocean-scene-color` copy 1920×1080 | 0.16 | 0.18 | 0.17 | <1% |
| shadow map 2048² | 0.13 | 0.15 | 0.15 | <1% |
| SMAA ×3, bloom mips ×7, god rays, clouds, composite | each <0.12 | | | ~1% total |

Post is NOT the problem: the whole post chain after the scene pass is ~1% of
busy. Everything worth cutting is inside the one scene pass.

### 2b. Inside the scene pass — frozen-frame A/B

Sim held with `loop.setFixedDt(0)`, chase camera, pack, 554 draws, 3.35 M tris,
GPU busy ≈ 26 ms. Legs interleaved with the baseline either side (both series
drift upward over a run). Deltas under ~1.5 ms are noise.

| knob | ΔGPU busy | ΔCPU | Δdraws | share of busy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **ocean surface hidden** | **−9.7** | −4.1 | −220 | **37%** |
| **spray / vesselFx off** | **−8.1** | +0.1 | −6 | **30%** |
| 7 rivals hidden | −3.3 | −3.0 | −266 | 12% |
| pixelRatio 1 → 0.75 (56% of the pixels) | −4.2 | 0.0 | 0 | 16% |
| god ray scale 0.5 → 0.25 | −1.6 | +2.6 | 0 | 6% |
| whole post chain off | −0.7 | −0.4 | −21 | 3% |
| **planar reflection live off** | **−0.3** | **−3.5** | **−219** | 1% |
| course furniture hidden | noise (+3.6) | −2.5 | −151 | — |
| shadows off | −0.07 | — | 0 | <1% |

Two results decide the whole optimisation pass.

1. **W9 spray is now 30% of the frame.** At T205 `vesselFx` spray computes were
   0.13 ms and ambient spray 0.05. The at-speed hull-spray and rooster-tail
   pools that landed since cost **8 ms of fill** on a pack frame, for six draw
   calls — this is overdraw in the particle pools, not draw-call or compute
   cost. It is the single largest NEW cost since the baseline.
2. **The planar reflection is a CPU tax with no GPU price.** 219 draw calls and
   3.5 ms of main-thread JS to produce 0.3 ms of GPU work. It re-renders
   essentially the whole scene into a 960×540 target, and 40% of every frame's
   draw calls are that second pass.

`furniture hidden` read +3.6 ms of busy, which is physically impossible and is
the noise floor asserting itself on a leg whose real GPU cost T205 measured at
1.4–1.8 ms. Its CPU and draw-call deltas (−2.5 ms, −151 draws) are consistent
and are the numbers to use.

The `shadows off` leg reported 3128 ms of CPU: setting `castShadow` at runtime
throws inside three r180's `updateShadow` every frame. Same three fault T205
recorded; the GPU delta (−0.07) is still readable and shadows are cheap.

MSAA could not be A/B'd at runtime — `renderer.samples` is read once by
`PassNode.setup`, so it needs a reload. Measured separately in §4.

## 4. What changed

### Quality tiers, as they now ship

`QUALITY_BUNDLES` in `src/ui/settingsStore.ts`. `?cine` is unchanged: it boots
`high` with every feature on, in memory only, so the trailer path still shoots
at maximum.

| | low | **medium (default)** | high / `?cine` |
|---|---|---|---|
| scene pass MSAA | 0 | **0** | 4 |
| canvas MSAA | off | off | 4× |
| resolutionScale | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| shadow map | 1024 | 2048 | 4096 |
| foliage density | 0.35 | 0.75 | 1 |
| reflections / caustics / deck water / spray / rain / shadows | off | **on** | on |
| post chain (bloom, god rays, grade, SMAA) | off | **on** | on |
| measured, pack chase | 56.5 fps | **59.1 fps** | 30.2 fps |

Where a player reaches this. The speedboat's pause menu carries a **Graphics**
row that cycles Low/Medium/High only — the full per-switch settings screen
(`src/ui/settingsScreen.ts`, where the new **Edge smoothing** row sits beside
Resolution) is the pirate/raft page's. So on the speedboat the tier is the
control, and it now moves the biggest knob with it.

Known gap, pre-existing and shared with `shadowMapSize`: switching tier
mid-session does not restate MSAA, because the scene pass is allocated once.
The post pipeline warns to the console when the gate drifts, and the settings
screen's row shows the pending-reload marker, but the speedboat's one-line
Graphics row has nowhere to show it. Cycling quality then reloading is correct;
cycling it alone changes everything except the sample count.

`STORAGE_KEY` v2 → v3: a persisted graphics block beats a bundle edit forever,
so without the bump every existing player would have kept the 4× scene pass at
the default tier — i.e. exactly the 30 fps this pass exists to fix — with no
setting they could reach that said why.

### T214 — the scene pass stops paying for 4× MSAA

`postParams.msaaSamples`, handed to `pass(scene, camera, { samples })`. That is
the only place a sample count sticks: `PassNode.setup()` copies
`renderer.samples` (antialias:true → 4) onto the target on the first render and
overwrites anything assigned afterwards. It is a construction gate and is named
in the pipeline's drift list, so a later write says so instead of looking
applied. Low and medium build at 0; high and `?cine` keep 4.

**Single-variable A/B, same build, same tree, reboot per leg, mid-race pack
chase, 1920×1080** (msaa4 and msaa0 interleaved to absorb drift):

| leg | fps | frame p50 | GPU busy | CPU | draws |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MSAA 4 | 26.5 | 35.9 | 33.0 | 28.3 | 549 |
| MSAA 0 | 39.7 | 25.1 | **15.3** | 24.0 | 545 |
| MSAA 4 (repeat) | 27.8 | 33.9 | 31.8 | 24.6 | 542 |
| MSAA 0 (repeat) | 45.0 | 22.0 | **15.2** | 21.1 | 542 |

**GPU busy 32 → 15 ms (−52%), fps 27 → 42, at the same draw calls.** SMAA still
runs at the end of the chain, on the resolved image, so the edges it
reconstructs are the ones it reconstructed before.

#### MSAA 2 is not allocatable, and it fails by looking fast

Asking for `samples: 2` is rejected at texture creation on this machine:

```
[gpu] uncaptured error: The sample count (2) of the texture is not supported.
 - While validating [TextureDescriptor "output-msaa"].
```

and the page **kept running**. The depth target failed the same way,
`copyFramebufferToTexture` submitted an invalid command buffer, fragment
binding 15 failed validation — and the window reported **a steady 60.00 fps at
7.0 ms of GPU busy with 299 draw calls instead of 542**, because the scene pass
was drawing nothing. Read without the console, that is the best result in this
entire document.

WebGPU guarantees only sample counts 1 and 4 for a render attachment. 2 is now
offered nowhere: `MSAA_SAMPLES` is `[0, 4]`, `sanitizeSettings` snaps a
persisted value onto that set, `msaaSamples()` snaps a param write, and
`tests/ui.test.ts` holds all three with the reason attached.

### The ranking INVERTS once MSAA is off — re-measured, same method

Same frozen-frame A/B as §2b, re-run at `msaaSamples: 0`. GPU busy ≈ 10.3 ms,
CPU ≈ 9 ms, 495 draws. Baseline drift over the run was 10.2 → 13.7 ms, so
deltas under ~1.5 ms are noise and are marked as such.

| knob | ΔGPU (MSAA 4) | **ΔGPU (MSAA 0)** | ΔCPU | Δdraws |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ocean surface hidden | −9.7 | **−3.6** | −2.8 | −195 |
| **spray / vesselFx off** | **−8.1** | **−0.02** | −0.2 | −6 |
| pixelRatio → 0.6 | — | −3.8 | −0.3 | 0 |
| pixelRatio → 0.75 | −4.2 | −2.8 | 0.0 | 0 |
| shadows off | −0.07 | −2.8 (drift-inflated base) | +0.2 | 0 |
| **planar reflection live off** | −0.3 | −0.6 | **−3.2** | **−194** |
| 7 rivals hidden | −3.3 | noise (+0.2) | −2.2 | −219 |
| course furniture hidden | noise | noise (+0.6) | −1.9 | −135 |
| god ray scale 0.5 → 0.25 | −1.6 | **0.00** | +0.05 | 0 |
| whole post chain off | −0.7 | noise (+3.5) | −1.1 | −21 |
| reflection RT 0.5 → 0.25 | +0.2 | −0.08 | 0.0 | 0 |

**The at-speed spray was never 8 ms of spray.** It was 8 ms of 4×-multisampled
alpha blending — the pools are thousands of large soft overlapping sprites, and
every blend was paying 4× the tile traffic. With MSAA off the entire spray
system measures **−0.02 ms**, i.e. free. **No spray pool budget was changed, no
particle count was lowered, and the W9 at-speed look is untouched** — the thing
that looked like the second-biggest cost in the frame was a symptom of the
biggest one.

The same correction applies to the god rays (−1.6 → exactly 0.00) and to the
rivals' GPU cost (−3.3 → noise). Optimising any of the three off the first
table would have spent quality for nothing.

**What is actually left, in order:**

1. **Draw calls, on the CPU.** The planar reflection re-renders ~194–219 objects
   into a 960×540 blurred target for 0.6 ms of GPU and 3.2 ms of main thread —
   about 40% of every frame's draw calls. Rivals (219) and furniture (135) are
   the next two, and all three are CPU-only now.
2. **Ocean surface shading**, −3.6 ms, still the largest single GPU item.
3. **Pixel count**, −2.8 ms at 0.75 — now a proportionally larger lever than it
   was, because the fill it scales is no longer swamped by MSAA.

A note on the reflection, since it is the obvious next target and the obvious
approach does not work: three's `ReflectorNode._updateVirtualCamera` does
`virtualCamera.projectionMatrix.copy(camera.projectionMatrix)` and then replaces
the third row with the oblique clip plane, so the virtual camera's own `far` is
never used and a cheap distance cull on the mirror is not available through it.
Cutting its draws means taking objects out by layer
(`excludeFromReflection`) — which is a look decision, not a free one, and is
left for a follow-up rather than guessed at here.

### The canvas was multisampling too, and it made `low` the slowest tier

`antialias` on the WebGPURenderer is a DIFFERENT buffer from the post chain's
HDR scene pass. With post ON it is redundant — post composites through a
full-screen quad, so there is no geometry edge on the canvas to resolve. With
post OFF, which is exactly what the `low` tier does, it is the only target.

So after T214, `low` measured **30.6 fps at 29.1 ms of GPU busy** against
medium's 59.1 at 14.1: the tier that exists to buy frames back was the only one
still paying for 4× multisampling. `App.create` now takes an `antialias` option
(default `true`, so `index.html` and `raft.html` are bit-identical) and the
speedboat page drives it from the same tier knob. **Low: 30.6 → 56.5 fps.**

The price at `low` is that it now has no antialiasing at all — no MSAA and, with
post off, no SMAA either. That is the honest meaning of the tier, and a low-tier
player can still switch post on mid-session (`postFx` is `reload: false`) to get
SMAA back for ~1 ms of CPU.

### Against the targets

- **≥60 fps at 1080p in the race at the default quality: MET in the pack**
  (chase 59.1, cockpit 59.9, near 59.6, free roam 60.0 — all at the 16.66 ms
  ceiling), **MISSED on the two widest framings**: far chase 50.5 and the
  rolling start 47.6. Both are GPU-side (18.1 and 17.4 ms busy) with the CPU
  within a millisecond of the same number, so neither wall alone explains it.
- **≥30 fps at maximum quality: MET in 4 of 6 windows** (30.1–32.7) and missed
  in cockpit and far chase at 26.6.

### Machine state, and why some rows are noisier than others

Every number above is headless Chrome on the M3 Max with the GPU lock held, but
the machine is shared and was not equally quiet throughout:

- The **before** table and the **after / medium and high** tables were taken
  while only the race-AI agent's CPU-only sweep was running.
- A later repeat of the medium rows, taken while `mediaanalysisd` (56%), two
  other agents' `node` processes (68% and 53%) and macOS's animated-wallpaper
  extension were live, read **GPU busy unchanged (14.5–17.5 ms) but CPU up from
  14.7 to 20–27 ms**, and fps down to 35–48. That is the contention signature,
  not a regression: the GPU column is the one to trust across sessions, and it
  did not move.
- The **before** run was taken before T213 landed; the after runs are on
  T213+. The MSAA and canvas A/Bs were both single-variable on ONE build, so
  they are unaffected by that drift; the before/after table above is not, and
  the two widest framings in particular should be re-taken on a quiet machine
  before anyone tunes against them.

## 6. What is still expensive

In order, all measured at the shipped default:

1. **Draw calls on the CPU, led by the planar reflection** — ~194–219 draws and
   3.2 ms of main thread for 0.6 ms of GPU, about 40% of the frame's draw
   calls. Rivals (219 draws, 2.2 ms) and course furniture (135 draws, 1.9 ms)
   follow. `render submit` tracks draw calls at ~0.028 ms/draw.
2. **Ocean surface shading** — 3.6 ms, the largest single GPU item, and the
   reason the far chase and the rolling start are the two framings that miss
   60: both put more water and more island on screen.
3. **The CPU ocean mirror** — 4.3–6.8 ms per frame inside the sim tick, and
   essentially all of `frame.tickSea`. Untouched here on purpose: §V110 says the
   player and the NPCs must feel the same sea, so this is a physics-parity
   change, not a render one.
4. **Pixel count** — −2.8 ms at pixelRatio 0.75. Deliberately NOT spent:
   `resolutionScale` stays 1 at every tier. The existing measurement in
   `settingsStore.ts` (RMSE 15.83 vs 10.76 against a 2×SSAA reference) is
   against downscaling, and stacking a blurrier framebuffer on top of a scene
   pass that just lost its MSAA is the wrong order to spend quality in.

Explicitly NOT worth touching, measured: the spray/vesselFx pools (−0.02 ms),
the god rays (0.00 ms), the reflection's RT resolution (−0.08 ms), the shadow
map (0.13–0.15 ms), and the whole post chain after the scene pass (~1% of busy).

## 8. Reproducing this

The harness is scratch-only and is not checked in — the same mistake T205 made,
and for the same reason (it is ~600 lines of throwaway CDP glue). What matters
and is NOT obvious is written down above: use the interval UNION rather than a
sum; key the frame clock off the rAF callback that submitted GPU work, because
three registers a second rAF beside the page's loop and counting both reports a
p50 of 0.15 ms; serve an rsync snapshot on your own port so another agent's
edits cannot move the numbers mid-run; and seed `localStorage` before the first
script runs so an A/B leg differs in exactly one graphics field.
