# Terrain + vegetation research (T111, R3.5)

Scope: why the Sierra islands read as "Playmobil", which techniques fix that inside our constraints (WebGPU three.js TSL, compute allowed, procedural-only, seeded/deterministic, §V17 compute ≤ 4 ms / render ≤ 8 ms at 1440p), and a ranked T112 plan. Art direction: Ghibli colour, Firewatch/Olly Moss composition, painterly shaders possibly later (§V94). Code audit is read-only; nothing here was edited.

## 1. Gap analysis of our code

### 1.1 What the R0 frames show

`docs/raft2100/lookdev/R0/noon.png`, `dawn.png`: islands are a row of smooth ellipsoid blobs (the boulder instances) on a flat tan disc with a few 8-sided cones. Nothing in the silhouette says "rock", "ridge" or "drainage"; the island has the same value everywhere, so distance is unreadable; the far island (left, ~1.5 km) is as sharp as the near one (aerial perspective is not applied on the LOD path, already noted in `lookdev/R0/params.md`). The ocean beside it has directional structure at three scales; the land has structure at none. That contrast is the whole complaint.

### 1.2 Heightfield (`src/island/heightmap.ts`, `src/island/sierraArchetypes.ts`)

- CPU, per-sample, no passes. `generateIslandHeightmap` (`heightmap.ts:436-664`) evaluates, per texel, an analytic landmass (`sierra.landAt`) + 3-octave coast fbm (`:534`) + 4-octave detail fbm (`:538`) + beach apron (`:304-324`) + lagoon basin + rim envelope + peak-floor rescale. Every term is isotropic or a monotone remap of height. There is no domain warp, no ridged/billow noise, no erosion (hydraulic or thermal), no drainage, no curvature, no talus, no jointing in the height data.
- Noise is value noise (`src/terrain/noiseCpu.ts:52-90`, mirrored in `src/terrain/noise.ts:38-66`), lacunarity 2, gain 0.5. Value-noise fbm on its own looks like a blurred cloud; it cannot produce a ridgeline or a gully.
- Resolution: `sliceGridSize: 256` for R = 150-280 m (`src/params/sierra.ts:150-154`), i.e. 1.2-2.2 m per texel; fillers and the Half Dome at 128 (7.5 m/texel on R = 480).
- Exfoliation is `terraceSteps`, a height quantisation inside one angular sector (`sierraArchetypes.ts:132-142, 179-187`), 2-4 steps of 5 m. Real sheeting follows the convex surface (see §2.2), not sea level; quantising by height produces contour lines, i.e. a wedding cake.
- The drowned ridge and cirque are `smoothMax` of radial `pow(1-d/r, p)` segments (`src/island/archetypes.ts:63-130`): a ridge made of overlapping cones has a scalloped crest and no spurs.
- Walkability/clue path are not authored anywhere in `src/`; they are asserted after the fact by a BFS in `tests/sierra.test.ts:258`. `sierraSites.ts` does site layout only. Nothing in the heightfield knows where the player is meant to walk, so there is no corridor, no worn ground, no fork.
- Good: deterministic `createRng`, byte-identity tests, `heightAt`/`gradientAt` (`heightmap.ts:630-696`) as the one truth for buoyancy, pines, cover and structures. Any new pass must run on the `data` grid before upload so those stay consistent.

### 1.3 Mesh (`src/island/islandMesh.ts`)

One vertex per texel (~65 k), skirt + 8 seabed apron rings, `computeVertexNormals()` (`:204`), stride-2 LOD past 900 m. No displacement, no height/normal texture, no detail normal. At 1.5 m per vertex with smooth normals, every surface is a lit pillow.

### 1.4 Materials (`src/island/sierraMaterial.ts`, `src/terrain/sandMaterial.ts`, `rockMaterial.ts`, `groundCover.ts`)

- Sierra is a recolour of the pirate `terrainBlendMaterial` (`sierraMaterial.ts:97-157`, `sandMaterial.ts:915-1056`): three layers picked by `normalWorldGeometry.y` against `slopeThreshold 0.72 ± 0.1` with a 2-octave jitter. Slope is the only mask.
- **The cover layer is never retinted** (`applyTones`, `sierraMaterial.ts:139-146`). On a dome nearly everything is under 35°, so the dome is painted with the pirate tropical green 18 m fbm (`groundCover.ts:121-135`, `terrainParams.vegScale 0.055`). That is the "naked unstructured terrain texture", and it is the wrong biome as well.
- Rock: tri-planar 3-octave fbm at 6 m base / 1.5 m finest, posterised into 5 bands, a second fbm "cavity" (`rockMaterial.ts:87-153`). No geometry-derived cavity/curvature, no detail normal, no macro variation.
- "Jointing" is two fixed world-space stripe sets on `positionWorld.xz` (`sierraMaterial.ts:50-51, 81-90`), identical azimuth on every island, not aligned to anything.
- No AO on terrain: GTAO wired but off (`src/params/post.ts:64`); sun shadow is one 80 m ortho map centred on the ship (`src/params/sky.ts:640-641`), so a 250 m island has no self-shadow beyond the raft's neighbourhood. Sierra boulders use the pirate rock material (`islandMaterials.ts:77`), so they do not match the ground they sit on.
- Good: band-limiting (`src/ship/bandLimit.ts`) is used consistently; `reliefNormalFromScreenGradient` (`src/ship/surfaceRelief.ts`) already drives the sand ripple normal (`sandMaterial.ts:644-647`) and is the template for a terrain detail normal; `seabed.ts` already bakes the CPU heightfield to a world-space GPU texture (`seabed.ts:10-16`), which is the hook for a terrain-info texture.

### 1.5 Rocks (`src/island/rocks.ts`)

Four instanced families of a deformed icosahedron (good geometry, ~720 tris). Placement is shore-ring only (`findShoreRadius ± 16 m`, `:160`), ~60 boulders + ~11 cliff masses at R 250. Nothing inland, no talus under the terraces or cirque walls, no size sorting with slope.

### 1.6 Vegetation (`src/vegetation/pineGeometry.ts`, `pineScatter.ts`, `windSway.ts`)

- Trees are 3-4 closed 8-sided cones on a 6-sided trunk (`pineGeometry.ts:98-115`), flat per-role colour, roughness 0.9, no backlight (`setSunDirection` is a no-op, `pineScatter.ts:301-303`).
- Scatter: `gradientAt ≤ tan 28°` and `h ≥ 2.5 m`, 5 stand centres with ±34 m jitter, species by a fixed 0.3 fraction. No aspect, elevation, moisture, drainage or rock-proximity logic, no footprint competition.
- Counts are tiny: `pinesPerRadius 0.28` × bench fraction gives ~12 trees on the cirque and ~5 on the ridge (`sierra.ts:203-215`).
- Understory is the pirate grass tufts + shrub domes unchanged (`island.ts:249-253`, `groundCoverMesh.ts:277-326`). No manzanita, litter, deadfall or scree. Cover casts no shadow.
- Good: `windSway.ts` (bend ∝ weight², along-normal flutter) is a fine base; instanced batches with sway attributes are the right architecture.

## 2. Techniques

### 2.1 Macro form: erosion and structured noise

**Why.** Real terrain is the trace of water and gravity: ridgelines are drainage divides, gullies are accumulated flow, talus is gravity-sorted debris at a repose angle. Noise has none of these correlations, which is what the eye detects as "cloud", not "land". Every technique below adds one correlation.

1. **Structured noise first (cheap, deterministic, CPU or GPU).** Quilez domain warping (`h = fbm(p + 4·fbm(p + 4·fbm(p)))`, https://iquilezles.org/articles/warp/) breaks the isotropy; de Carpentier's Swiss/Jordan turbulence (https://www.decarpentier.nl/scape-procedural-extensions) uses analytic noise derivatives so finer octaves are damped and warped by the accumulated gradient, which gives glaciated-ridge and gully looks from noise alone. Cost: 4-8 octaves with derivatives, trivially fast at 512². Replaces value noise with gradient (simplex/Perlin) noise with analytic derivatives; `noise.ts`/`noiseCpu.ts` mirror contract must be kept.
2. **Thermal erosion (Musgrave, Kolb, Mace 1989, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/74333.74337).** Per cell, move a fraction of the height excess above `T = cellSize·tan(repose)` to lower neighbours. Converges to slopes ≤ repose; this IS the talus apron/cone. Run on a separate debris layer fed by a rockfall source ∝ cliff steepness × joint density, with bedrock keeping its own steeper threshold (Šťava et al. 2008 layering, https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/resources/papers/Stava-2008-Interactive_Terrain_Modeling_Using_Hydraulic_Erosion.pdf). Axel Paris' GPU notes (https://aparis69.github.io/public_html/posts/terrain_erosion.html): use the double-buffer variant for determinism; < 0.1 ms per iteration at 512². 200-500 iterations is a one-time bake.
3. **Stream-power / uplift (Cordonnier et al. 2016, https://hal.science/hal-01262376; Schott et al. 2023, https://hal.science/hal-04049125, code https://github.com/H-Schott/StreamPowerErosion).** Iterate `dh/dt = U - k·A^m·|∇h|^n` where A is drainage area; ridges emerge as divides, basins over-deepen. This gives the drowned-ridge and cirque-lagoon macro structure without authoring it as cones. A WebGPU port exists (TerrainX, https://github.com/GPU-Gang/WebGPU-Erosion-Simulation, 80+ fps at 1201²) and is the closest drop-in WGSL reference. Grid version is atomic-free, fixed-order, therefore seed-reproducible. Hundreds of iterations at ~1 ms at 512² = bake over load, not per frame.
4. **Virtual-pipes hydraulic erosion (Mei, Decaudin, Hu 2007, http://www-evasion.imag.fr/Publications/2007/MDH07/FastErosion_PG07.pdf).** 256² in 1.29 ms/step, 512² in 2.38 ms on a 2007 GPU; deterministic if rain is hashed per cell. Produces dendritic soft-sediment gullies. Use only on the regolith/grus layer, never on bedrock, or granite turns into badlands. Sebastian Lague's droplet method (https://github.com/SebLague/Hydraulic-Erosion) is simplest on CPU (70 k droplets at 256² well under a second) but its compute version races and is not deterministic; CPU only for us.
5. **Feature-curve diffusion (Hnaidi et al. 2010, https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/eric.galin/Articles/2010-terrain.pdf).** Ridges, cliffs and paths are vector curves with elevation and slope constraints; the heightfield is a guided Laplace diffusion solved by GPU multigrid (0.19 s at 512² in 2010). This is the formal version of "path polyline constrains the heightfield" (§2.3).
6. Learned sketch-to-terrain (Guérin et al. 2017 cGAN) is excluded: trained weights = non-procedural.

**Determinism note.** Sin-based hashes diverge across GPUs and `floor(float)` hashing diverges at large coordinates (https://danilw.github.io/blog/Hash_Noise_in_GPU_Shaders/). Use integer hashes (lowbias32/PCG) on integer lattice coordinates seeded from the world seed; keep bakes atomic-free with ping-pong buffers. Our CPU/GPU noise mirror (`noise.ts`/`noiseCpu.ts`) already obeys this in spirit; the erosion bake can live on CPU at 512² (tens of ms per 100 iterations in a typed-array loop, run in a worker or chunked over frames) which sidesteps GPU readback entirely and keeps `heightAt` authoritative. Recommendation: **CPU bake at 512², GPU bake only if profiling demands it.**

### 2.2 Granite-specific form

- **Sheeting/exfoliation joints form on convex surfaces** (Martel 2006, https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2005GL024710): tensile stress ∝ curvature × slope-parallel compression; sheets 0.5-10 m thick near the surface, spacing growing with depth. Recipe: compute mean curvature of the bedrock field; where curvature > 0, quantise height measured **along the local normal** into 1-3 m onion steps with plate-wise noise offsets (Gaea Stratify/Terrace in shader form, https://docs.quadspinner.com/Reference/Erosion/Stratify.html); concave areas get none. That is the difference between Lembert Dome and a wedding cake.
- **Roche moutonnée / whalebacks** (Krabbendam & Glasser 2011, https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/14105/1/Krabbendam_Glasser_Glacial_Erosion_2011.pdf; Lembert Dome, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lembert_Dome): a per-island ice-flow vector; stoss flank low-passed and polished, lee flank stepped at joint spacing and steeper. Wide joint spacing = whaleback, close spacing = angular plucked face. A joint-density noise picks the regime per cell. Half Dome's face follows one major vertical joint: straight cliff faces come from orthogonal joint sets, rounded tops from sheeting.
- **Talus** rests at 33-37° (tan 35° ≈ 0.70), fall-sorted with the largest blocks at the foot, cones radiating from joint chutes merging into aprons. Implement as the debris layer in §2.1(2) plus instanced boulders sampled from the debris thickness map with size increasing downslope.
- **Glacial polish and erratics**: polish = roughness pull on up-facing convex bedrock (we have the roughness knob, `sierraMaterial.ts:135-137`, but no curvature mask); erratics = a few large isolated boulders on polished treads (cheap, instant scale cue).

### 2.3 Playability-first authoring (path graph → heightfield)

- **Galin et al. 2010 "Procedural Generation of Roads"** (https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/egalin/Articles/2010-roads.pdf): anisotropic shortest path with a cost penalising slope (climbing along the gradient costs more than traversing), curvature, water; then **excavate and embank the terrain along the path**. This is literally "author the path, carve the corridor". Cost: Dijkstra on a 128² downsample is milliseconds; the carve is one pass.
- **Hnaidi 2010** (above) formalises the carve as a constraint diffusion, which blends better than a stamp.
- **Breath of the Wild, GDC 2017 "Change and Constant"** (https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024562/Change-and-Constant-Breaking-Conventions; analysis https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/breath-of-the-wild-open-world-analysis-gravity-to-go-forward): triangles at three scales (large = landmark, medium = occluders that hide the reveal until you crest, small = texture) and "gravity" (visible, curious objects pulling the player off-route). Our mapping: the Half Dome is the large triangle; each island needs one medium ridge placed **across** the suggested path so the fork payoff (a clue piece, a dead-tree stand, a lit dome) is hidden until the crest.
- **Firewatch** (Anderson/Rodkin "Designing for Exploration and Choice" https://gdcvault.com/play/1022108/Designing-for-Exploration-and-Choice; "Making the World of Firewatch" https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023191/Making-the-World-of): hub of trails, soft gating (chokepoints, blocked trails), constant landmark (the tower), views composed at trail bends. Soft gates for us = corridors whose cross-profile is 40-45° (slide back, no climb) vs main path < 30°.
- **Journey** (Chen, "Designing Journey" https://gdcvault.com/play/1017700/Designing): downhill = forward. Give each island a low-frequency tilt so "downhill" agrees with "toward the next station".
- **Desire lines** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desire_path): render the path mask as compressed litter, bare grus and broken manzanita rather than a decal; lower grass density, fewer trees inside the corridor.

**Proposed authoring model.** Per island: a seeded path graph (landing → station(s) → exit, plus ≥ 1 fork to a distraction POI) built on the macro heightfield with anisotropic A*; each edge carries corridor width and a target slope profile (main ≤ 30°, fork ≤ 33° with one 35° "scramble" step, barrier ≥ 45°); a carve/diffusion pass writes the heightfield; a **path-distance texture** is emitted for shading, placement and the "jungle" fork (shrub density high beside the fork, low on the main route). The BFS test in `tests/sierra.test.ts:258` then becomes a property of authored content instead of luck.

### 2.4 Vegetation that reads alive

- **Horizon Zero Dawn, GDC 2017 "GPU-Based Run-Time Procedural Placement"** (https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn, vault https://gdcvault.com/play/1024700/GPU-Based-Run-Time-Procedural): per-ecotope density maps from slope/height/masks, blue-noise dithered into placements, footprint radius so large assets suppress smaller ones. Maps one-to-one onto a TSL compute pass reading our terrain-info texture (§2.5) and writing an `instancedArray`; deterministic because the threshold is blue noise sampled by world position. Frostbite's shader splatting (Andersson 2007, https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/terrain-rendering-in-frostbite-using-procedural-shader-splatting-presentation/916086) makes the key point: **placement and texturing share the same masks**, so where the shader paints litter, the placement puts pines.
- **Ecosystem competition** (Deussen et al. 1998, https://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/ecosys.sig98.pdf; Makowski et al. 2019 "Synthetic silviculture", https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3306346.3323039): place, kill the smaller of overlapping footprints, fill gaps with pioneers, 2-3 rounds. This yields size-graded clusters, the single strongest "alive" cue. Sierra specifics: Jeffrey pine in soil pockets (concave, moist, < 25°), juniper on bare convex rock and windward edges, manzanita in dense sunny patches on bare ground (the shrub "jungle" for forks), dead snags along the drowned crest (already have `emitTreeline`).
- **Geometry**: replace cones with a branch-whorl tree (trunk + 5-8 whorls of 4-6 branch cards of needle clusters; 600-1500 tris near, reduced whorl depth mid, impostor far). Juniper = twisted trunk + dense round crown; manzanita = red smooth multi-stem + small leathery leaf clusters.
- **Lighting**: spherical/cluster normals (normal = normalize(worldPos − clusterCentre) blended ~70/30 with the geometric normal, https://halisavakis.com/my-take-on-shaders-stylized-tree-leaves/) give Ghibli blob grouping without card flicker; add wrap + a back-transmission term `pow(saturate(dot(-L, V)), k) · thickness` (UE Two Sided Foliage model, https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/shading-models-in-unreal-engine). Same sun/sky/fog inputs as the ocean; only the BRDF shape is stylised, so land and water stay in one world.
- **Wind**: hierarchical (Pivot Painter 2 idea, https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/pivot-painter-tool-2.0-in-unreal-engine) but since we build the tree in code, emit `branchPivot/branchDepth/branchPhase` attributes and nest three rotations in the vertex shader. Ghost of Tsushima's wind talk (https://gdcvault.com/play/1027124/Blowing-from-the-West-Simulating) and grass talk (https://gdcvault.com/play/1027033/Advanced-Graphics-Summit-Procedural-Grass): one global wind source read by grass, trees, shrubs and (ours) ropes/sails, so motion is coherent. Our `windSway.ts` plus the existing weather wind field is the source.
- **Understory and litter**: needle litter and grus as shader layers driven by pine density and slope; deadfall logs and cones as small instanced meshes; bunchgrass only where moisture is high (drainage accumulation + near waterline). Understory casting shadows matters less than pines casting onto understory.
- **Impostors and culling**: octahedral impostors baked **at runtime** from the procedural trees into a render target (keeps the no-image-texture rule; Amplify manual for the maths https://wiki.amplify.pt/index.php?title=Unity_Products%3AAmplify_Impostors%2FManual); compute frustum + hi-z culling writing `IndirectStorageBufferAttribute` draw args per LOD bucket (three.js r17x+). Plausible budget: 0.2 ms cull, 1.5-2.5 ms foliage draw at 1440p.

### 2.5 Texturing and shading

- **Terrain-info texture** (one compute pass at bake, or CPU alongside the heightfield since `seabed.ts` already uploads the field): slope, aspect, mean curvature, horizon-based AO, flow accumulation/moisture, path distance, debris thickness, joint density. Everything downstream reads it.
- **Masks → layers**: height-blend stack (Far Cry 5 "Terrain Rendering" https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/presentations/TerrainRenderingFarCry5.pdf; GPU Gems 3 ch.1 tri-planar https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-i-geometry/chapter-1-generating-complex-procedural-terrains-using-gpu): polished granite on convex + low slope; sheeted granite on convex + steep (banding aligned to the curvature direction, not world axes); fractured granite on high joint density; grus/DG on gentle concavities; needle litter under pine density; wet dark band above waterline; bright lichen on north aspects. Blend by `w = saturate((h_layer + mask − maxOverAll + width)/width)` with procedural per-layer heights. Macro variation: 2-octave noise at 40-80 m modulating value ±10 % to kill the uniform read; micro: a band-limited detail normal via `reliefNormalFromScreenGradient` as in the sand ripples.
- **AO/self-shadow**: horizon mapping (Max 1988; Snyder & Nowrouzezahrai 2008 https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/fast-soft-self-shadowing-dynamic-height-fields/): precompute max horizon angle per cell in 8-16 azimuths once (microseconds at 512²), shade sun self-shadow and sky occlusion from it. Cheaper and stabler than extending the 80 m shadow map to 300 m, and it works at 1.5 km where the silhouette needs a dark lee. Keep GTAO for contact between boulders/trees and ground (turn it on, measure).
- **Stylisation**: Firewatch (Jane Ng, "The Art of Firewatch" https://gdcvault.com/play/1022295/The-Art-of) gets its painted look mostly from value grouping by distance (fog bands per elevation/time) and silhouette-first shapes, not from a paint filter; our terrain material can stay flat in value and let aerial perspective (once applied to the LOD path, D13) do the work, which is also what keeps it compatible with the PBR ocean. Sea of Thieves (SIGGRAPH 2018 https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3214745.3214820) and Kena stylise by colour ramps and simplified shading, sharing one light rig. If a painterly pass comes later: anisotropic Kuwahara in TSL (Heckel, https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/on-crafting-painterly-shaders/; Kyprianidis https://www.kyprianidis.com/p/pg2009/) costs ~1-2 ms at 1440p and should be masked to land/foliage; running it over the ocean destroys the specular structure that sells the water.

### 2.6 Rendering budget

- Heightfield: CDLOD (Strugar, https://aggrobird.com/files/cdlod_latest.pdf) or a 3-ring clipmap (Losasso & Hoppe https://hhoppe.com/proj/geomclipmap/) with per-vertex morph removes the stride-2 pop. Far Cry 5 spends 1.5-2 ms on full-screen terrain G-buffer; with 3 islands and a walking camera, ≤ 2 ms is realistic at 512² per island. Going to 512² (D14) quadruples vertex count to ~262 k per island at stride 1, which is why LOD must come with it; alternatively keep 256² meshes and sample a 512² height texture for displacement near the camera.
- Everything in §2.1-2.2 is a **bake at island creation**, not per frame, so §V17's compute budget is untouched in steady state. Chunk the bake over frames or a worker so load does not stall.

## 3. Ranked plan (T112 sub-tasks)

Order = value per effort with dependencies respected. Impact = effect on the "Playmobil" read, 1-5. Tests are §V80-style properties on CPU data (heightmaps, placement lists), no GPU.

**T112a — Quick wins tier (first visible jump).** Goal: remove the cheapest, loudest toy cues in one pass. Approach: (1) retint the cover layer in `applyTones` and replace the 18 m tropical green with a granite/grus/litter palette driven by slope + a 60 m macro noise; (2) sierra rock material for boulders (`islandMaterials.ts:77`); (3) turn GTAO on and measure; (4) bake an 8-azimuth horizon map on the CPU grid and feed sun self-shadow + sky AO into the terrain and rock materials; (5) `sliceGridSize` 512 with a stride-2 far LOD and morph; (6) raise pine counts 5-10× and cluster by 2 rounds of footprint competition using existing `gradientAt`; (7) boulders inland on convex/steep cells + a few erratics on treads; (8) apply aerial perspective on the distant island LOD path (D13). Files: `src/island/sierraMaterial.ts`, `islandMaterials.ts`, `heightmap.ts` (horizon bake), `islandMesh.ts`, `src/vegetation/pineScatter.ts`, `src/island/rocks.ts`, `src/params/sierra.ts`, `src/params/post.ts`. Effort: M. Impact: 3. Tests: horizon angles monotone in azimuthal sweep and 0 on a flat field; cluster statistic (mean nearest-neighbour distance < uniform-random expectation); boulder count on convex cells > 0; determinism hash of placement. Risk: 512² quadruples mesh cost (mitigated by LOD); GTAO cost unknown until measured.

**T112b — Path-first island authoring with distraction forks.** Goal: the walkable route is content, not luck; each slice island has a main route landing → station → exit and ≥ 1 fork to a distraction POI through a shrub patch. Approach: seeded path graph on a 128² downsample with anisotropic A* (Galin 2010 cost), Catmull-Rom splines, a carve/diffusion pass (Hnaidi-style blend, corridor width 3-6 m, main ≤ 30°, fork ≤ 33° with one 35° scramble, barriers ≥ 45°), medium "occluder" ridge placed across the route before the payoff (BotW), low-frequency tilt toward the next island (Journey), a path-distance channel in the terrain-info texture used by shading (worn grus) and placement (no trees on route, manzanita dense on forks). Files: new `src/island/pathGraph.ts`, `src/island/heightmap.ts` (carve pass), `sierraSites.ts` (station/fork POIs), `src/params/sierra.ts`. Effort: L. Impact: 4 (and it is the narrative requirement). Tests: ∀ main-route polyline sample, slope < 35° (replaces the BFS test as a property of authored data); ≥ 1 fork whose end is ≥ N m from the route and not visible from the landing (line-of-sight over the heightfield); fork shrub-density > route density; barriers around station ≥ 45° somewhere on every non-route bearing; seed determinism. Risk: carving fights the erosion pass (order: macro → erosion → carve → thermal smoothing of carve edges); corridor stamps can look like roads if the blend is too crisp.

**T112c — Erosion and macro form.** Goal: ridges, spurs, gullies, sheeting and talus instead of cones + fbm. Approach: replace value fbm with gradient noise + analytic derivatives and Swiss/Jordan turbulence + domain warp in `noiseCpu.ts`/`noise.ts` (mirror kept); bedrock pass: curvature-masked sheeting along the normal, stoss/lee asymmetry from a per-island ice vector and joint-density noise; debris layer with rockfall source + Musgrave thermal erosion at tan 35° (ping-pong, CPU 512², chunked); optional stream-power bake (TerrainX kernel) for the drowned ridge and cirque if the archetype shapes still read as cones; flood at sea level. `heightAt` stays the truth. Files: `src/terrain/noiseCpu.ts`, `noise.ts`, `src/island/heightmap.ts` (new pass stage after `rawHeight`), `sierraArchetypes.ts` (sheeting replaces `terraceSteps`), `src/params/sierra.ts`. Effort: L. Impact: 5. Tests: after thermal pass, max slope on debris cells ≤ repose + ε and total mass conserved; sheeting steps present only where curvature > 0; stoss mean slope < lee mean slope along the ice vector; byte-identity for a fixed seed; existing §V90 beach-continuity and Half-Dome distance tests still pass; pirate islands' hashes unchanged. Risk: the biggest risk in the plan; erosion parameters are easy to overdo (badlands); CPU bake time at 512² × 3 islands must stay < ~300 ms or go to a worker.

**T112d — Terrain-info texture and layered shading.** Goal: the ground reads as granite with a history. Approach: bake slope/aspect/curvature/AO/moisture/path/debris/joint channels (CPU, uploaded like `seabed.ts`); height-blended layer stack with curvature-aligned sheeting bands (replacing the world-space stripes), fractured/polished/grus/litter/wet/lichen layers, macro variation, band-limited detail normal via `reliefNormalFromScreenGradient`, roughness from polish mask. Files: `src/island/sierraMaterial.ts` (becomes its own material rather than a retint), `src/terrain/rockMaterial.ts`, new `src/island/terrainInfo.ts`. Effort: M-L. Impact: 4. Tests: texture channels in range; AO = 1 on a flat field, < 1 in a pit; sheeting band direction follows curvature principal direction on a synthetic dome; band-limit gate reaches 0 at sub-pixel (existing `bandLimit` tests). Risk: §V48 speckle if any band is unlimited; material cost at 1440p with 6 layers × tri-planar, gate tri-planar to steep fragments.

**T112e — Living vegetation layers.** Goal: canopy, understory, litter that cluster ecologically and are lit like foliage. Approach: species density maps (pine: concave/moist/< 25°; juniper: convex/bare/windward; manzanita: sunny/bare/fork corridor; snags: drowned crest) from the terrain-info texture, blue-noise threshold + 2-3 rounds of footprint competition (CPU at bake, deterministic); new whorl-branch pine, juniper, manzanita geometry; cluster normals + wrap + back-transmission in TSL; hierarchical wind from `windSway.ts` attributes; deadfall/cones/bunchgrass small instances; litter/grus shader layer keyed to pine density; pine shadows on understory. Files: `src/vegetation/pineGeometry.ts`, new `juniperGeometry.ts`, `manzanitaGeometry.ts`, `pineScatter.ts` → `sierraScatter.ts`, `windSway.ts`, `src/terrain/groundCoverMesh.ts` (sierra understory set), `src/params/sierra.ts`. Effort: L. Impact: 5. Tests: species/mask correlation (pine mean moisture > juniper mean moisture; manzanita density on fork > on route); no instance inside the route corridor; footprint overlap = 0 after competition; counts scale with bench area not with R; determinism hash. Risk: draw cost; do LOD (T112g) before pushing counts past ~3-5 k instances per island.

**T112f — Outcrops and talus as geometry.** Goal: meso rock where the heightfield implies it. Approach: sample the debris-thickness channel for talus boulders with size increasing downslope; outcrop slabs on convex + high joint density cells aligned to the sheeting direction; cirque wall blocks at the cliff foot; rock material = sierra material (shared masks). Files: `src/island/rocks.ts`, `islandMaterials.ts`. Effort: M. Impact: 3. Tests: talus instances only where debris > threshold; median boulder size at the apron foot > at the head; no boulder on route corridor. Risk: low.

**T112g — LOD without pop.** Goal: 512² terrain, thousands of plants, no pops. Approach: CDLOD-style morph on the island mesh; runtime-baked octahedral impostors for trees; compute frustum + hi-z cull writing indirect draw args per LOD bucket. Files: `src/island/islandMesh.ts`, new `src/vegetation/impostor.ts`, `src/vegetation/cull.ts`. Effort: M-L. Impact: 2 directly, but gates T112e counts. Tests: morph is continuous at the LOD boundary (height delta < texel); cull keeps all instances inside a synthetic frustum. Risk: three.js indirect-draw API churn between releases.

**T112h — Painterly pass (decision-gated, after R3 lookdev).** Goal: Ghibli/Firewatch surface quality if the user chooses it. Approach: value grouping via aerial perspective bands per elevation and time first (free once D13 is fixed); then optional anisotropic Kuwahara at 8 sectors, radius 4-6, masked to land + foliage via a stencil/ID buffer, never over the ocean. Files: `src/core/postPipeline.ts`, `src/params/post.ts`. Effort: M. Impact: 3 (style), 0 on structure. Tests: perf regression gate only. Risk: kills fine specular; must be masked.

Suggested sequence: **T112a → T112b → T112c → T112d → T112e → T112f → T112g → T112h**, with T112b and T112c developed against the same new pass stage in `heightmap.ts` so they merge cleanly; T112d and T112e can run in parallel once the terrain-info channels are defined (define the channel layout first, as a shared interface, before fanning out).

## 4. Open questions for the user

1. **Painterly vs PBR-stylised.** Firewatch gets its look mostly from value grouping (fog bands) and silhouette, not a paint filter; Sea of Thieves/Kena keep PBR and stylise colour. A Kuwahara pass is ~1-2 ms and must be masked off the ocean. Decision: stylise through masks/ramps/aerial perspective only (T112a-e), or budget a masked painterly post now (T112h)?
2. **Jungle/shrub density on forks.** Manzanita thickets in the Sierra are chest-high and near-impassable; a "jungle" fork can be (a) a dense but walkable corridor of manzanita + juniper with the route visible through gaps, or (b) a genuinely occluding thicket you push through (needs player-foliage collision/bending). (a) is cheap; (b) is a gameplay feature.
3. **Explicit trails vs implied.** Worn grus + compressed litter + cairns (implied), or an authored visible trail ribbon with switchbacks (explicit, Firewatch-like)? Implied fits the meditative/“nobody has been here since the flood” fiction; explicit is more legible.
4. **How wild may the macro form get.** Stream-power erosion will deform the archetype silhouettes (dome, drowned ridge, cirque) away from their current analytic shapes. Keep the archetypes as uplift masks and let erosion own the silhouette, or keep the analytic silhouettes and add erosion as detail only?
5. **512² per slice island vs a 512² height texture with displacement on a 256² mesh.** The first is simpler; the second is cheaper; both need LOD morph.
6. **Pirate islands.** Adopt T112a/d for free (palette aside), or freeze them (their hashes are pinned in tests)?
7. **Bake budget.** Is ~0.3-1 s of CPU bake at load (chunked) acceptable, or must everything be GPU compute with readback?
