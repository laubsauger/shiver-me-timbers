Raft 2100

A balsa raft, a radio, and a drowned mountain range. You sail toward voices.

A first-person ambient exploration game. The Kon-Tiki raft carries you between the high ground of a flooded Sierra in the year 2100, following fragments on shortwave to islands where the recorded voice of Alan Watts is waiting in pieces.

01 · The idea

Slow is the point.

There is no combat, no enemy, no failure. The raft makes three knots downwind and cannot point into the wind. You steer by raising and lowering planks between the logs, the way Heyerdahl did. Waiting is part of the design.

The reward is a voice. Each island carries one idea from Alan Watts, assembled from fragments you find on foot. Some parts only resolve later, back on the raft, at night, on the radio. That is the reason to leave again.

The game never explains a word of it. It puts a tiller in your hands and lets a voice arrive while you are busy steering.Design principle · the teaching is never a cutscene

02 · The loop

Listen, dial, sail, land, walk, return.

One cycle is 20–30 minutes. The first playable has three islands, three ideas, and a raft that becomes home.

raft
Listen

Static, then fragments: a sentence, a laugh, a bell.

cabin
Dial in

Tune by hand. Signal strength is directional; sweep the heading and the static thins toward the source.

deck
Sail

A marker on the horizon. Guaras, steering oar, sheet. Music and weather carry the crossing.

shore
Land

Beach the raft, step off the bow onto granite sand.

island
Walk

Cairns, a dead orchard, a drowned lookout. Three to five clue spots, one idea.

night
Return

The idea's last piece plays on the raft after dark. Sleep, or don't. Dawn brings a new fragment.

03 · The raft

A real vessel, to the centimetre.

Built from Heyerdahl's own account and the museum raft in Oslo: nine balsa logs, a woven bamboo cabin you have to crouch to enter, a bipod mast with the painted sun-god face on the sail, five steering planks, a six-metre oar. Every station is a place you walk to. Nothing is a menu.

04 · The voice

Alan Watts, in cooperation with his family.

We are working with the Alan Watts Organization and Mark Watts on the selection and use of archival recordings. The game is built as a framework: clips, texts and their places in the world live in a manifest, so the curated material drops in without touching code.

Watts is never explained or gamified. A fragment lands on you while your hands are busy with a tiller or a dial. The world is the frame; the voice is the picture.

  • Proximity audio from objects on the islands: a bell in a tree, a half-buried speaker, a shell.
  • Found text: carved planks, a notebook under a rock, a painted sign.
  • Night callbacks on the raft radio that close each idea away from its island.
  • Original ambient score (koto, shakuhachi, soft synth) for the voyages; the score ducks fully for the voice.
05 · The look

Ghibli light on Sierra granite.

A physically based sea, sky and sun, graded per hour of the day. Far islands flatten into two or three tones the way a screen print does; near things are generous and saturated. Whimsical, not cute. The flood happened long ago; the world has settled into it.

dawn · peach over slate, fog between islands
noon · clean cyan, hard shadows, white granite
dusk · amber to violet, rim light
night · indigo, a moon road on the water, one lantern
06 · Why we can build it

The engine already floats.

Raft 2100 runs on our WebGPU engine built for a sailing combat simulation: a spectral FFT ocean, volumetric clouds, a physically based sky with sun and moon, compute-solved rope rigging, a membrane sail model, procedural islands with beaches, HRTF spatial audio, deterministic fixed-step simulation. It runs in a browser, on a link, with nothing to install.

The raft blueprint, its sailing model and the first-person walker landed this week; materials and hand stations are in progress. What remains is the world, the radio and the content plumbing.

  • Ocean, sky, clouds, weather — shipping today.
  • Procedural vessels — three classes share one piece contract; the raft is the third.
  • Islands — generator with beach apron, terraces, lagoons; Sierra archetypes next.
  • Audio — spatial emitters, two-deck music player, ducking; the radio reuses it.
  • Browser-first — playable from a URL; ideal for press, partners and the Foundation.
