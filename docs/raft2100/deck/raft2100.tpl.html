Raft 2100

A balsa raft, a radio, and a drowned mountain range. You sail toward voices.

Ambient first-person exploration on the Shiver Me Timbers engine. The Kon-Tiki raft, rebuilt to its 1947 bones, carries you between the high ground of a flooded Sierra in the year 2100, following fragments on the radio to islands where Alan Watts is waiting in pieces.

01 · Concept

What this is, and what it is not

Raft 2100 is a second game mode in the same repository. It shares the ocean, sky, clouds, islands, ropes, audio and post pipeline with the pirate simulation and shares none of its intent. There is no combat, no enemy, no failure state. The pace is set by a raft that makes three knots downwind and cannot point into the wind. Waiting is part of the design, not a cost of it.

The content is the recorded voice of Alan Watts (1915–1973), supplied through our cooperation with the Alan Watts Organization and Mark Watts. The game does not explain Watts. It places you somewhere quiet and lets a three-minute fragment land on you while you are doing something else with your hands: holding a tiller, tuning a dial, walking a beach at dusk. One island, one teaching, assembled from scattered pieces.

Slowness is the mechanic.The raft's real handling limits (downwind-ish, guaras and a steering oar, no reverse) are the source of the mood. We tune toward accessible, not arcade. A harder "true Kon-Tiki" toggle exists for those who want it.
Everything is touched, nothing is clicked.In play, every action is a place on the raft you walk to: the oar at the stern, the radio in the cabin corner, the guara planks in the chinks between logs, the sheet at the cabin wall. Hotkeys exist for debugging and for filming from outside cameras. They are not the experience.
The voice is the reward, the world is the frame.Teachings are not collectibles. Each island is built around one idea; the clues on it are the idea's parts, found in an order the terrain suggests. Some parts only resolve later, back on the raft, at night, through the radio. That is the reason to leave.
Plumbing first, content later.Every clip is a placeholder MP3 behind a manifest. Curated Watts material drops in without code changes once chosen. We spend zero time on clip selection now.

Tone

Whimsical but not cute. Japanese-inspired in its restraint and its reverence for weather: Ghibli's water and light (Ponyo's sea, Mononoke's forests on the high ground) for colour and generosity; Firewatch and Olly Moss for how a landscape is reduced to layered planes and a palette per hour of the day. No post-apocalypse grime. The flood happened a long time ago; the world has settled into it.

02 · Core loop

Listen, dial, sail, land, walk, return

One full cycle is 20–30 minutes: a short sail, an island, a night on the raft. The vertical slice has three islands and three teachings, arranged so that each voyage crosses a different kind of water and a different hour of the day.

1 · raft
ListenStatic on the radio. Fragments drift in and out: a sentence, a laugh, a bell. Each fragment belongs to a station somewhere in the archipelago.
2 · cabin
Dial inTurn the tuning knob until a fragment steadies. Lock it. Signal strength is directional: sweep the raft's heading and the static thins toward the source.
3 · deck
SailA marker settles on the horizon and on the paper chart. Raise a guara forward, lower one aft, lean on the oar. Voyage music and weather fill the crossing. No teachings during the sail.
4 · shore
LandBeach the raft or anchor off. Step down onto sand. The raft stays where you left it, tugging at its line.
5 · island
WalkFollow the island's own logic (a path of cairns, a dead orchard, a drowned lookout) through 3–5 clue spots. Proximity audio, found objects, readable text.
6 · night
ReturnSome parts only complete on the raft after dark, on the radio. You sleep. Dawn brings a new fragment. Repeat.

Why each part is there

  • Directional radio replaces a quest marker with a skill. You learn to read static the way you read wind.
  • No teachings while sailing keeps the voyage a voyage: music, weather, the raft creaking. If Watts played during transit the islands would have no reason to exist.
  • Raft-radio callbacks at night close each island's idea away from the island, turning the raft from vehicle into home.
  • Three islands is the smallest number that creates a route, a choice of order, and a sense of archipelago.
  • Arrival is beaching. The raft grounds on the beach apron; you step off the bow. Push off to leave. No anchor ritual.
  • Night is optional. Lie on the mat and dawn comes; stay up and the night plays in real time. The radio callback plays either way.
03 · World

The high ground of the Sierra, 2100

Loose inspiration, not a digital elevation model. One exception: a single Half-Dome-like silhouette stands on the far horizon, unreachable, for anyone who knows the valley. The flood is deep enough that the Central Valley is open sea and only the granite high country stands: domes, benches, cirques, a few drowned conifer ridgelines with their tops above water. Recognisability is not a goal; Sierra character is. Glacial-polished granite, Jeffrey pine and juniper on the benches, manzanita and snowbrush lower, a beach where the old treeline meets the new waterline.

The existing archipelago generator (src/island/: archetypes, beach apron, terraces, lagoon basin, sea stacks, headlands) already produces islands with a beach band. For this mode we author a new archetype family: granite dome (smooth, round-shouldered, exfoliation terraces), drowned ridge (long, narrow, a line of tree tops just breaking the surface to leeward), and cirque bowl (a half-ring enclosing a still lagoon). Palms become pines; sand becomes decomposed granite with a drift line.

Realism dial

We keep the engine's physically-based ocean, sky and sun (it is a real asset here) and apply the stylisation in three places only: terrain and vegetation forms, a colour grade per hour, and atmospheric layering (fog banks between islands that Firewatch-flatten the far planes). That gives a Ghibli-bright close world on a believable sea without a full NPR rewrite.

04 · The raft

Kon-Tiki, 1947 bones, 2100 dressing

The structure is the real raft at true scale, from Heyerdahl's own account and the museum raft in Oslo (full reference with citations: docs/raft2100/kon-tiki-reference.md). The dressing is re-imagined for 2100: same crates in the same places, different contents. The Kon-Tiki face stays on the sail; it is the one piece of 1947 the fiction keeps on purpose.

Walkable, to scale

Eye height 1.65 m standing, 1.05 m crouched; the cabin forces the crouch on entry and releases it at the door. Walkable surface = the raft's own heightfield: logs (round, so footing slopes toward the chinks), deck mats (flat, proud of the logs by the cross-beam height ≈ 0.30 m), cabin floor (mats over boxes, another 0.35 m up). Moving between these is stepping, not climbing. The deck edge is a real edge: you can walk off it into the sea; the raft drifts slowly and is always catchable, and you climb back at the foot-rail. There is no death state.

Your hands are in frame. In the first build they are simple low-poly mitts with a grab pose, enough to prove every station; sculpted hands and per-station animation come in the polish phase. The parrot's cage hangs under the roof, empty, door open.

Interaction stations, all diegetic and all placed where the real crew stood: tiller (stern, on the bare logs), five guaras (grab a plank, pull up or push down), sheets (cleats on the cabin's forward corners), halyard (mast foot), radio (cabin, port-aft corner, behind the partition), lantern (aft gable), chart (cabin, starboard wall by the door), sleeping mat (cabin), ladder (climb to the lookout platform), gangway (any deck edge within 1 m of ground = step ashore).

05 · Radio

The dial is the map

The radio is a diegetic object you kneel in front of. One tuning knob, one volume knob, one signal meter, one speaker, a hand crank. There is no menu.

Stations

Each island broadcasts on a fixed frequency from a fixed world position. A station has a fragment pool (3–6 short audio pieces, placeholder MP3s), a locked marker, and a night pool (the callbacks). Stations do not move.

Tuning

Audible signal = clarity(Δf) × strength(d, bearing). Clarity is a narrow window around the station frequency; strength falls with distance and is multiplied by a cardioid on the raft's heading relative to the station (the aerial runs fore-aft along the mast, so the fiction holds). Static fills the remainder of the gain. Below a clarity threshold you hear words through noise; above it for two seconds, the station locks: a soft chime, the meter holds, and a marker appears on the horizon haze and on the paper chart.

Night

After dusk, locked stations whose island has been visited open their night pool; the radio, left on, plays one callback per night. Propagation is better at night (true of shortwave, and a useful excuse): range roughly doubles.

Implementation surface

  • src/radio/ (new): stations.ts (data + manifest), tuner.ts (pure: frequency, bearing, distance → clarity/strength), radioAudio.ts (static synth + two-deck fragment player on the existing music bus, HRTF-positioned at the radio prop).
  • Marker rendering: a sprite in the sky haze layer plus a chart. The chart is a toggled overlay first (phase 4) and becomes a paper prop on the cabin wall in the polish phase.
  • Tests: tuner purity and the cardioid (heading toward station ⇒ strength up), lock hysteresis, night-range doubling.
06 · Islands & teachings

One idea per island

A teaching is an idea, not a lecture. The island's form, its props and its clue order all serve that one idea. Content is placeholder until the Foundation's clips are chosen; the structure is built now.

Clue types (all three in the slice)

  • Proximity audio. A source object (a bell in a tree, a half-buried speaker, a shell, a cairn) plays a fragment spatially when you approach; repeats on re-approach after a cooldown.
  • Found objects + text. A carved plank, a notebook under a rock, a painted sign. Diegetic, readable by picking up. Short.
  • Raft-radio callbacks. The idea's last piece. Plays only at night on the raft after the island's clues are found.

Data model

On what stays when the ground is gone. A narrow ridge; the clues are in the standing dead pines; one bell in a tree you reach by wading.

On water that takes the shape of whatever holds it. A still lagoon inside a half-ring of granite; you pole the raft in; the last clue is read from the raft.

07 · Art direction

Ghibli light on Sierra granite

Two touchstones, one job each. Ghibli (Ponyo, Mononoke) decides colour: generous, saturated, clean water blues and leaf greens, warm skin-light on wood. Firewatch / Olly Moss decides composition: a palette per hour, far planes flattened by haze into 3–4 tones, bold silhouettes, sun always placed for a rim. The engine's physically-based sea, sun and clouds stay; the grade and the landscape forms do the stylising.

Grading pipeline

The post pipeline today ends in ACES + vibrance + grain + SMAA; there is no LUT or lift-gamma-gain stage. We add one grade node after tonemapping (slot at postPipeline.ts:289): a 3D LUT (32³) per hour band, blended by time of day, plus a global lift/gamma/gain for quick iteration. LUTs are authored in-engine from screenshots of each hour, not imported from film stock. Vignette stays; grain drops to near zero (grain reads as grit, and this world is not gritty).

Atmosphere

  • Fog as a device: banks between islands, so a destination appears as a silhouette first, then a tone, then a place. Weather presets reuse the existing spatial weather field with the storm presets removed.
  • Far planes flatten: haze strength biased up so distant islands collapse to 2–3 values. Near planes stay full-range.
  • Sun placement: the day clock is tuned so that the scripted arrival times at each slice island fall at a flattering hour.

Typography in-world

Signs and the chart use a hand-lettered brush face; the radio's dial plate uses a worn DIN-like engraving. No UI typography in play except the settings screens.

08 · Audio

Three layers, never silent

  1. Bed: the existing ocean/wind bed and procedural fallback, plus raft-specific loops (rope creak on load, bamboo deck flex, water through the chinks, sail luff). Reuses src/audio/bed.ts.
  2. Score: an original ambient score (koto, shakuhachi, soft synth drones; bespoke MP3s, Suno allowed). Situations: 'voyage' | 'approach' | 'island' | 'night'; the existing playlist selector already has 'island' and an unwired landDistance hook. Score ducks fully for any Watts clip.
  3. Voice: Watts fragments. Spatial (HRTF) when from a prop or the radio; dry only for the night callbacks heard lying in the cabin. Placeholder MP3s now; manifest swap later.

Asset convention: assets/audio/teachings/<teaching>/<clue>.mp3, credited in CREDITS.md, discovered by the same build-time glob as music.
