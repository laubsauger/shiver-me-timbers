# Technical Design Document: Sea of Thieves Water Generation Logic

This document details the systems and logic behind the stylized, dynamic water rendering in *Sea of Thieves*, derived directly from Rare's technical art presentation [[00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=0)]. It is structured to provide a technical agent with the necessary logic to implement an equivalent system in WebGL/Three.js, focusing heavily on simulation logic, Framebuffer Object (FBO) usage, and shader manipulation rather than engine-specifics.

*(Note: Direct image generation is disabled in this environment. Instead, precise timestamps and detailed descriptions of the key visual diagrams from the presentation are provided so the implementing agent can reference them directly).*

---

## 1. Ocean Water Simulation (FFT)

The ocean relies on realistic physical simulation combined with stylized shading to bridge the gap between believable fluid dynamics and the game's painterly art style [[02:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=131)].

### Base Simulation

* **Logic:** The base wave simulation relies on an FFT (Fast Fourier Transform) implementation based on Jerry Tessendorf's 2001 paper, "Simulating Ocean Water" [[04:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=290)].
* **Stylization Choice:** The team attempted to smooth the geometry and specular highlights to make it more "stylized," but found that highly detailed, realistic wave motion looked better. They retained the high-frequency FFT geometry and achieved their art style strictly through shading and foam behavior [[05:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=322)].

### Subsurface Scattering (SSS) Approximation

* **Logic:** They do not use a physically accurate SSS model. Instead, they use the "choppiness" vertex offsets (generated by the FFT simulation) to create a mask that isolates the *sides* of the waves [[06:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=388)].
* **Implementation:** They take the Dot Product of the `Light Vector` and the `View Vector`. This result is combined with the choppiness mask to apply an artificially boosted SSS glow strictly to the sides/peaks of the waves, creating that signature bright, translucent stylized water look [[06:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=402)].

## 2. Dynamic Foam Generation

Foam is not a static texture; it is an active simulation that responds to the wave overlap and weather conditions [[06:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=408)].

### The Jacobian Determinant

* **Logic:** Foam is generated dynamically by evaluating the Jacobian determinant of the wave transform [[06:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=413)].
* **Trigger:** When the choppiness offsets cause a wave peak to overlap itself, the Jacobian term goes negative. Where it goes negative, foam "pixels" are injected into a render target that tiles across the ocean surface.
* **Bias:** To get more foam, they simply bias the Jacobian determinant downward. During storms, they increase the wave amplitude and apply a heavier negative bias to generate massive patches of foam [[08:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=498)].
* **Reference Illustration [[07:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=435)]:** *The presentation displays a graph showing the chopped wave surface (blue area) and the Jacobian (yellow line). Red dots are plotted wherever the yellow line dips below the zero-axis, marking exactly where foam spawns.*

### Progressive Blurring for Stylization

* **Problem:** The raw Jacobian foam output is incredibly noisy and photorealistic, breaking the game's art style [[07:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=447)].
* **Solution:** The foam render target is **progressively blurred** frame-by-frame [[07:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=456)].
* **Effect:** At the exact moment of a wave peak, the foam crest is sharp. Over the next several frames, the blur causes the foam to visually dissipate and spread out naturally without needing complex particle fluid physics.
* **Texture Blending:** The resulting blurred mask is blended with artist-authored textures [[07:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=471)]. A high-frequency stylized texture is mapped to the immediate wave crests, which mathematically fades into a lower-frequency (softer) texture as the mask blurs out.

## 3. Shallow Deck Water Simulation

When waves crash over a ship, water pools on the deck and sloshes around dynamically based on the ship's rotation [[09:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=547)].

### Data Structure

* **Heightfield:** The deck of the ship is represented by a simple top-down 2D heightfield texture (rendered to a small 512x192 render target). This captures the overall slope as well as small crevices between the wood planks [[09:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=575)].
* **Assumption:** The deck is treated as "leaky," meaning the volume of water doesn't need to be perfectly preserved, drastically simplifying the math [[09:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=563)].

### Two-Pass Hydraulic Erosion (Mei et al. 2007)

The system runs a very cheap 2D fluid simulation on the GPU, passing water from higher elevation pixels to lower elevation neighbor pixels [[09:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=582)].

* **Pass 1 (Outflow) [[10:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=604)]:** Calculates how much water leaves a cell. *Crucial logic:* They take the normal of the ship's surface (which is rotating due to the waves) and use it to bias the outflow. This causes the water to dynamically slide back and forth across the deck as the ship rocks.
* **Pass 2 (Inflow) [[10:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=622)]:** Gathers the water flowing in from neighboring cells and subtracts the outflow.
* **Channel Packing:**
* `Blue Channel` = The current volume/depth of the water.
* `Green Channel` = Persistent wetness. If a cell has water, wetness is added here.

* **Evaporation:** A small constant is subtracted from both channels every frame, ensuring water drains away and the deck eventually dries [[10:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=641)].
* **Material Update:** A normal map is generated from this simulation and used to interpolate the deck's material properties (lowering the roughness, boosting specular) where water is present [[10:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=657)].

## 4. Flowing Water, Waterfalls, & Intersection Foam

To handle water intersecting with solid objects (like rocks in a waterfall or a ship cutting through the ocean), they bypass complex geometry in favor of a clever depth-mapping trick [[11:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=700)].

### Depth Intersection Masking

* **Logic:** They render the intersecting mesh (e.g., a waterfall plane) to an off-screen render target, using the mesh's *unwrapped UVs* as the screen position [[11:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=717)].
* **Depth Comparison:** For every pixel, they check its distance from the camera against the main scene depth buffer (rendered previously). If the distance of the water plane pixel is very close to the depth of the solid object behind it, they mark that pixel with a value [[12:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=736)].
* **Result:** This instantly creates a pixel-perfect intersection mask around any object touching the water, with built-in depth occlusion (e.g., a wooden bridge in front of a waterfall will correctly mask out the water behind it) [[13:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=832)].
* **Reference Illustration [[12:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=758)]:** *A diagram showing a camera projecting a ray at a scene with a flat red water plane and a white intersecting pillar. The off-screen render target accurately maps the point of intersection between the two objects based purely on their proximity in the depth buffer.*

### Advection & Flow

* **Logic:** To make the intersection foam move, the intersection mask is additively blended with the previous frame's result [[13:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=808)].
* **Flow Map:** The accumulation is then advected (pushed) and blurred using a scrolling, low-frequency flow map texture (noise) [[13:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=813)].
* **Effect:** This causes foam to continuously generate at the point of intersection and trail off smoothly behind moving objects or flow down waterfalls [[14:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=850)].
* **Reference Illustration [[13:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9BOz2dFZzs&t=790)]:** *Shows top-down test footage of foam accumulating at the base of grey pillars, successfully advecting and stretching downward along the designated flow map vectors.*

### Implementation Notes for Three.js Agent

To replicate this in WebGL/Three.js, rely heavily on `WebGLRenderTarget`.

1. The **FFT simulation** should be handled in a dedicated ping-pong FBO.
2. The **Foam Blur** requires a ping-pong FBO to accumulate and blur the Jacobian output iteratively.
3. The **Deck Water** maps directly to a GPGPU `GPUComputationRenderer` setup, ping-ponging the heightfield texture to calculate outflow and inflow using the camera's local rotation to bias the vector.
4. The **Intersection Foam** can be achieved by reading the `depthTexture` from your main scene `WebGLRenderTarget`, comparing it inside the fragment shader of your water plane, and writing the intersections to an accumulation FBO that samples an advection noise map.

Here is the updated technical design document, re-architected entirely for **WebGPU** using Three.js's modern `WebGPURenderer` and the Three Shading Language (TSL).

Moving from WebGL to WebGPU fundamentally shifts the paradigm. Instead of relying on expensive GPGPU fragment-shader hacks (like `GPUComputationRenderer`) and ping-pong Framebuffer Objects, this architecture leverages native **Compute Shaders**, **Storage Buffers**, and **Storage Textures** to handle the heavy mathematical simulations required by the art style.

# Technical Design Document: Sea of Thieves Environment Systems (WebGPU Architecture)

**Target Environment:** Three.js (`WebGPURenderer`), TSL (Three Shading Language), WGSL
**Reference System:** Unreal Engine 4 (Deferred Renderer)

## 1. Ocean Water Simulation (FFT & SSS)

The Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) ocean simulation is a perfect candidate for WebGPU parallelization.

### Compute-Based FFT

* **WebGPU Implementation:** Instead of executing the Inverse FFT via multi-pass fragment shaders, utilize a TSL `compute()` node.
* **Execution:** A compute shader is dispatched (`compute().dispatch(resolution, resolution)`) to run the FFT algorithm across the complex wave spectrum. The resulting heightmap and horizontal displacement vectors ("choppiness") are written directly to a `StorageTexture` or a `storage()` buffer.
* **Stylization:** High-frequency geometry is maintained. The stylized look is entirely shader-driven.

### Subsurface Scattering (SSS) Approximation

* **Visual Reference:** [▶ View Frame at 06:42](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://youtu.be/y9BOz2dFZzs%3Ft%3D402)
* **WebGPU Implementation:** Inside the TSL material node for the water mesh, sample the choppiness displacement buffer. Calculate the dot product of the light and view vectors (`dot(lightDir, viewDir)`). Multiply this result by the choppiness mask to inject a high-intensity watercolor glow exclusively into the wave peaks and steep sides.

## 2. Dynamic Foam Generation (The Jacobian Determinant)

Foam is generated dynamically based on the wave mathematics folding over itself, rather than using particle physics.

### Jacobian Calculation & Progressive Blurring

* **The Math:** [▶ View Frame at 07:15](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://youtu.be/y9BOz2dFZzs%3Ft%3D435)
* **WebGPU Implementation:**

1. **Injection:** Inside the FFT compute pass, calculate the Jacobian determinant of the wave transformation. Where the value goes negative (meaning the wave is cresting/folding), the thread writes a foam value to a dedicated Foam `StorageTexture`.
2. **Blur Compute Pass:** [▶ View Frame at 07:36](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://youtu.be/y9BOz2dFZzs%3Ft%3D456) Because the raw math is too photorealistic and noisy, a secondary Compute Shader is dispatched every frame to apply a Gaussian blur to the Foam `StorageTexture`. This forces the foam to physically spread and dissipate over time.
3. **TSL Blending:** In the fragment stage, this blurred texture mask drives an interpolation (using `mix()`) between two artist-authored textures: a high-frequency crest texture and a softer, low-frequency dissipated texture.

## 3. Dynamic Deck Water (Shallow Water Erosion)

The 2-pass hydraulic erosion model handles water sloshing across the ship's deck. In WebGPU, this eliminates race conditions inherent to WebGL texture ping-ponging.

### 2D Fluid Compute Pipeline

* **Visual Reference:** [▶ View Frame at 09:35](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://youtu.be/y9BOz2dFZzs%3Ft%3D575)
* **Data Structure:** The deck's slope and crevices are mapped to a 2D grid `StorageBuffer` (e.g., 512x192 cells).
* **Pass 1 Compute (Outflow):** A compute shader reads the current water volume of each cell. It takes the ship's physical rotation vector (passed as a `uniform()`) to bias the gravity. The threads calculate how much water leaves their respective cells and write this to an intermediate Outflow Buffer.
* **Pass 2 Compute (Inflow & Resolve):** A synchronized compute pass reads the Outflow Buffer to gather incoming water from neighbors. It resolves the new volume (Blue channel) and persistent wetness (Green channel), subtracts a tiny constant for evaporation, and writes the final state back to the grid.
* **Material:** The deck's TSL material reads this buffer to dynamically lower roughness and alter the surface normal where water is pooling.

## 4. Intersection Foam & Flowing Water

To create dynamic foam around rocks and waterfalls without complex mesh collisions, the system uses a depth-buffer comparison. WebGPU makes depth access significantly cleaner.

### Depth Masking & Advection

* **Depth Masking:** [▶ View Frame at 12:38](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://youtu.be/y9BOz2dFZzs%3Ft%3D758) In the water plane's TSL material, directly sample the scene's depth buffer using `viewportDepthTexture()`. Compare the sampled scene depth against the fragment's computed depth. If the difference falls within a tight threshold, output a "1" to mark an intersection.
* **Advection Compute:** [▶ View Frame at 13:10](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://youtu.be/y9BOz2dFZzs%3Ft%3D790) Run a compute pass that reads the intersection data from the previous frame, samples a low-frequency noise texture (the flow map), and pushes (advects) the foam pixels along the noise vectors into the current frame's `StorageTexture`. This stretches the foam dynamically behind moving objects.

## 5. Volumetric Clouds (2.5D Compositing)

To avoid heavy volumetric raymarching, clouds are forward-rendered, blurred, and distorted.

### Depth-Scaled Blur & Quad Distortion

* **Compute Blur:** [▶ View Frame at 18:16](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://youtu.be/y9BOz2dFZzs%3Ft%3D1096) Cloud core geometry is rendered to an off-screen texture (packing Depth and Alpha). A compute shader applies a variable-width Gaussian blur, where the thread scales its sample radius based on the depth value of the pixel. Distant clouds soften differently than nearby ones.
* **Screen-Space Quad:** [▶ View Frame at 20:19](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://youtu.be/y9BOz2dFZzs%3Ft%3D1219) A quad is pinned to the camera frustum (~500m out). The TSL material for this quad samples the blurred cloud texture alongside a Cube Map of distortion noise to generate fluffy, animated edges.

## 6. Hyperbolic Ropes (Vertex Math)

Hundreds of rigging ropes dynamically deform without rigid-body physics by calculating Catenary curves (hyperbolas).

### WGSL Math Offloading

* **The Transcendental Equation:** [▶ View Frame at 28:19](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://youtu.be/y9BOz2dFZzs%3Ft%3D1699)
* **WebGPU Implementation:** While WebGL required running a 20-iteration numerical approximation loop inside the Vertex Shader for every single vertex every frame, WebGPU allows this to be entirely decoupled.
* **Compute Optimization:** Dispatch a 1D compute shader where one thread equals one rope segment. The thread evaluates the hyperbola parameter using WGSL's built-in math capabilities and calculates the exact curve based purely on Start Point, End Point, and Length.
* **Storage & Instancing:** The compute shader writes the final vertex positions, arc lengths, and normals directly into a `StorageBuffer`. The rendering pipeline simply uses a Three.js `BatchedMesh` or `InstancedMesh` that pulls its geometry data blindly from this buffer, completely freeing up the render pipeline from heavy math.
