# Swell shadow in island lee (T174)

## The gap

`src/ocean/fetchField.ts` bakes clear upwind distance and `src/ocean/fetch.ts`
turns it into per-cascade JONSWAP gains. That is a model of **wind sea**: waves
the local wind regrows once it has room. The **swell train**
(`swellPeriod`/`swellAmplitude`/`swellDirection`) was generated by a storm far
away. It does not regrow in a lee. Right now it passes through every island
unless the island also happens to lie upwind (fetch.ts header, "SWELL
BLOCKING"). The speedboat design doc names the same gap
(docs/speedboat/design-doc.md:43, Q9): flat lee chicanes need swell attenuation.

## What gets shadowed, and what does not

**Only the swell share of each cascade.** This is a deliberate choice, and it
departs from the first brief ("short cascade strongly shadowed"):

- Wind sea in a lee is already modelled, directionally, by fetch. The fetch
  law says short bands regrow within metres (their gain at small fetch is about
  1, or above 1 from young-sea steepening). If the swell shadow also removed
  those bands, two models would describe the same waves in the same place and
  disagree. That is the §V.77 failure, only this time in the physics.
- Wind sea runs along the wind, and swell does not (78° apart on the default
  sea). Shadowing chop along the swell bearing would lay a glassy wedge in a
  direction the wind ignores. With `swellAmplitude = 0` (glass preset) it would
  shadow along a bearing that belongs to no waves at all.
- In practice the swell lives in cascade 0 (by construction:
  `swellReferenceDomain`). So cascade 0 is the one that calms, and cascades 1
  and 2 get a gain of exactly 1.

Wavelength dependence still holds, but in the physical variable, which is the
swell's own wavelength. A 16 s swell (λ 400 m) wraps much further into a lee
than an 8 s swell (λ 100 m). The "long cascade barely shadowed by a small
islet, short strongly" intuition is the diffraction law K(k) below, and the
tests assert it on K.

## The model

Frame: ŝ = (cos θs, sin θs) is the swell's direction of travel, u = distance
downswell, v = lateral offset.

**1. Diffraction coefficient K(u, v)**: the ratio of swell amplitude to open-sea
amplitude. It is marched downswell row by row in a grid rotated onto ŝ:

    K(u+Δ, v) = (1 − L(u+Δ, v)) · [ G_V ∗ K(u, ·) ](v)
    V         = Δ · ( 2 σθ² · x  +  π / k )          lateral variance added per row
    x(u+Δ, v) = L ≥ ½ ? 0 : x(u, v) + Δ,  then  x ← min(x, x(v ∓ Δ) + 0.1·Δ) both ways along the row

- `L` is the land mask resampled into the rotated grid, with the same land test
  as fetch: seabed height ≥ 0.
- `x` is the distance to the nearest upswell land, counted as rows back plus
  0.1 × the lateral distance (a lateral distance transform run within each
  row). The lateral term has to be there: the blur *gathers* with each cell's
  own radius, so a lit column that has never "seen" land would otherwise take
  the largest radius and drag the shadow kilometres sideways. It also has to
  be small: with weight 1 a 300 m strip's deficit ∫(1−K)dv grew 389 → 701 m
  over 5 km; with weight 0.1 it goes 320 → 344 m, which is conserved to
  within the rasterisation.
- The march runs on cells 2 field texels wide (`SWELL_SHADOW_STRIDE`). That is
  the band limit rather than a shortcut, because the finished field gets the
  3-texel §V.48 blur anyway.
- Summed from the obstacle, the variance is **σ² = σθ²·x² + π·x/k**:
  - `π·x/k` is **diffraction**. √(πx/k) is the lateral offset where the Fresnel
    parameter v = y·√(k/πx) reaches 1. That is the width of the transition in
    Sommerfeld's half-plane (knife-edge) solution. A Gaussian blur of the
    binary shadow gives K = ½ on the geometric shadow line, which is the
    Sommerfeld / Penney–Price value at the breakwater shadow boundary. Using
    erf in place of the Fresnel integral drops the interference fringes, and
    any directional spread averages those out anyway (Goda et al. 1978).
  - `σθ²·x²` is **directional spreading**, which is ballistic: energy leaving at
    angle θ is displaced x·θ laterally. σθ = 1/√n for a cos^n θ spread, with
    n = `swellEffectiveDirectionality(p)`, the spread the grid actually draws
    (§V.8: shadow the sea that is drawn). Capped at σθ ≤ 1 rad.
- k = `swellPeakWavenumber(swellPeriod)`. The swell is narrow-band (σk/kp ≈
  0.12), so one k represents it.

Size dependence comes for free. An obstacle of width W refills once σ ~ W, so a
small islet (W < λ) is nearly transparent to long swell and a 1 km island casts
a long wake.

**2. Swell share per cascade.** The fraction of cascade i's height variance that
is swell. It is analytic, because the swell's radial density is Gaussian in k
(the 1/k in `swellSpectrum` cancels the k·dk Jacobian):

    fᵢ = clamp( A² · [Φ((kMaxᵢ − kp)/σk) − Φ((kMinᵢ − kp)/σk)] / Varᵢ , 0, 1 ) · strength

Here A = `swellAmplitude`, σk = `swellBandSigma`, and Varᵢ is the cascade's
measured `heightVariance` from the same spectrum snapshot on both sides.

**3. The one expression (§V.77)**, applied to cascade i's displacement next to
the shoaling and fetch gains:

    K'    = min( max(K, 0) / (1 − 10⁻³), 1 )           snap: bilinear/half-float of 1s reads 1 exactly
    gainᵢ = √( max( 1 − fᵢ·(1 − K'²), 0 ) )

Energy bookkeeping: the wind-sea part (1 − f) passes untouched, and the swell
part (f) is scaled by K². **Open water is bit-identical**: K' = 1 gives
1 − f·0 = 1 and √1 = 1, and so does f = 0 (no swell, strength 0, or cascades
1 and 2).

CPU `swellShadowGain` and TSL `swellShadowGainNode` live side by side in
`src/ocean/swellShadow.ts`, in the same pattern as `fetch.ts`.

## Where it lives

- **Bake**: `createFetchField` bakes K into the **G channel of the existing
  fetch texture** (R16F becomes RG16F). Same texture, same sampler, **zero new
  bindings** (§V.40). `fetchAt` and `shadowAt` read the same arrays the texture
  is packed from (§V.8).
- **GPU**: the vertex stage already samples the field for the fetch ratio. The
  varying widens from `float` to `vec2(ratio, K)`, so no new interpolant
  location and nothing extra in the fragment stage. The per-cascade `shoal[i]`
  product picks up `swellShadowGainNode`, so displacement, the Jacobian, the
  normals and the slope variance all carry it without further edits.
- **Terrain / caustics**: `waterHeightNode` (`caustics/causticsNode.ts`)
  multiplies the same `swellShadowGainNode` per cascade. Its share uniforms are
  refreshed by the same `swellBandShare` call, and K is the `.g` of the fetch
  sample it already takes (`Caustics.waterHeight`). This keeps the beach and
  the caustics on the drawn sea (§V.77). `tests/swellShadow.test.ts` walks that
  node graph and evaluates it.
- **CPU**: `CpuOcean.bandGains` multiplies the same gain per mirrored cascade,
  sampled at the grid coord `q` (§V.72a). `fᵢ` is refreshed on the same tick
  path as the fetch coefficients.
- **Rebuild** (§B119): `fetchAt`/`shadowAt` run the rebuild check against the live params before sampling, so the grid a tick reads always matches that tick's params. It re-marches K when the swell bearing moves more than
  `fetchRebuildRadians`, or when kp or σθ moves more than 2% relative. There is no call-count gate any more: a render-side gate made the sea hulls feel frame-rate dependent. Presets move the swell keys in 3 steps, so a preset change costs at most 3 re-marches of 8–12 ms. `update()` from render is usually a no-op; a `version` counter bumps on every rebuild.
  `swellShadowStrength` is a uniform and never rebuilds anything.
- **Band limit (§V.48)**: the diffusion is itself a low-pass, then the same
  `fetchBlurTexels` box blur runs. The field border is faded to K = 1 over
  `fetchFieldMargin/2`, so clamp-to-edge cannot carry a shadow out to the
  horizon.

## Not modelled (stated so it does not read as an error)

- **Refraction.** Swell bending around an island over its shelf, which is
  what fills the far lee of real round islands. Shoaling (§V.72) only
  attenuates by depth. It does not turn rays.
- **Fringes and reflection.** No standing-wave pattern off cliffs.
- **Wind sea in the lee.** That stays the fetch model, as explained above.
- **Directional averaging.** It is done on amplitude K, not energy K². This
  slightly under-shadows the penumbra, but K = ½ on the shadow line is kept.
## Sources

- A. Sommerfeld (1896), *Mathematische Theorie der Diffraction*, Math. Ann. 47:
  half-plane solution and Fresnel-integral form.
- W. G. Penney & A. T. Price (1952), *The diffraction theory of sea waves and
  the shelter afforded by breakwaters*, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 244: K = 0.5 on
  the shadow line.
- R. L. Wiegel (1962), *Diffraction of waves by semi-infinite breakwater*, J.
  Hydraulics Div. ASCE 88: the standard design diagrams.
- Y. Goda, T. Takayama & Y. Suzuki (1978), *Diffraction diagrams for
  directional random waves*, Proc. 16th ICCE: directional spread fills the
  shadow and removes fringes.
- A. C. Radder (1979), *On the parabolic equation method for water-wave
  propagation*, J. Fluid Mech. 95: the paraxial (march-downwave) approximation.
- L. H. Holthuijsen, A. Herman & N. Booij (2003), *Phase-decoupled
  refraction–diffraction for spectral wave models*, Coastal Eng. 49: the
  phase-averaged diffraction that SWAN uses, the same class of approximation as
  this.

Swell share f₀ of cascade 0, which is what gets shadowed: calm ≈ 1.0 (swell-
dominated), swell/default 0.28, storm 0.10, glass 0. f₁ = f₂ = 0 on every
preset. In the shipped worlds, 27% (pirate) and 20% (raft) of the field has
K < 0.9. The raft berths lie in the default swell's lee (drownedRidge K 0.05,
dome 0.12, cirque 0.35), so the raft now starts on calmer water. That is the
feature doing its job, not a regression.
